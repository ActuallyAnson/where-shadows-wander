# ADR 0011: Accessibility settings are one table, applied live, on a UIKit panel pressed by poke

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted (poke on a headset not yet verified) |
| Date | 13 Oct 2026 |

## Context

The GDD promises tolerance and hold-time settings, a shadow size, high contrast, captions and one-handed play, usable with hands only. The values already existed in code (`ShapeRecognizer.setTolerance` and `setHoldTimes`, `ShadowProjector.settings.size`, `ShadowSharpen.settings.strength`, the lantern flicker amplitude, the audio mixer), but nothing let a player change them, and nothing saved them.

## Decision

1. **One table** (`src/engine/services/settings/settings.ts`): every setting's key, range, step, default, label and one-line help. The panel, the saves, the repair code and the tests all read it. Defaults: tolerance 1, hold time 0.4 s (companions take 2.5 times as long, so 1 s, the GDD's values), shadow size 100 %, recognition glow 100 %, flicker 100 %, volumes as the mixer's own (master 1, music 0.7, effects 1, ambience 0.6), high contrast off, captions off, main hand right.
2. **One store** (`settings-store.ts`): holds the values, repairs each change into range, applies it at once to the engine objects it steers (through small shapes, so it is tested without three.js), and tells listeners. Applying is event-driven: nothing per frame.
3. **Saved in the save**, as version 2 ([ADR 0010](0010-saves.md)): a `settings` block and `settingsAt`. `main.ts` loads it at start and calls `saves.setSettings` on every change, so a change is written with the next save event.
4. **The lantern flicker follows the device until the player moves it.** `main.ts` picks 0.3 under the OS's reduced-motion preference and 1 otherwise; the store leaves the flicker alone until the player has chosen a value (or a non-default one is saved), and `?flicker=` still overrides everything for testing. Reset to defaults applies the table's default (1).
5. **The panel is an IWSDK UIKit panel pressed by poke** (`src/ui/settings-panel`).
   - *What IWSDK offers.* The spatial UI guide at `iwsdk.dev/guides/10-spatial-ui.html` is a 404; Meta's copy (`developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/iwsdk/guides/10-spatial-ui-uikitml/`) documents UIKitML, the `spatialUI` feature and `RayInteractable` only. The installed 1.0.0 code has more: `PanelUI` and `PanelDocument` components, a `PokeInteractable` tag with a `TouchPointer` that presses when the index fingertip crosses the surface, and horizon `slider` and `toggle` components.
   - *What we use.* `spatialUI` is turned on in `iwsdk.config.json` (it was off; turning it on changed nothing in the bundle, because UIKit is already inside `@iwsdk/core`, and the whole feature adds about 4 KB gzipped: 686 to 690 KB of the 800 KB budget. It does register IWSDK's panel, screen-space and follow systems, and the panel system walks the scene each frame to update documents, a small cost to measure on the Pico). The panel's markup is generated from the table as UIKitML text and loaded from a blob URL, so there is one source of truth and no file to keep in step. Controls are plain buttons: a `-` and `+` stepper per number and one button per toggle or choice, each at least 4 cm square at the panel's real size (0.62 m wide, 960 px). Sliders were not used: a fingertip cannot drag one precisely, and a stepper also reads out its value.
   - *Pinch.* Nothing: with the rays off, IWSDK's hand pinch is a ray gesture. A pinch-to-press path would need our own fingertip hit test; poke satisfies the requirement ("pinch or poke") and adds nothing to maintain.
   - *No controller path.* Rays stay off (`disableIwsdkRays`), the game has no controller code, and nothing about the panel needs one. Two caveats: the panel is also `RayInteractable`, only so mouse clicks work on a desktop (the hand rays are off, so it adds no way in on a headset), and IWSDK's touch pointer falls back to a controller's tip if one is held, which this game does not rely on or test.
6. **Details learned from the library.** UIKit's document is 1 px = 1 cm until scaled, so the entity is scaled to the panel's real width. A container's text is its parent's `text` property, so `setProperties({ text })` updates a value live and `setProperties({ display: 'none' })` hides a page. Class names are one global stylesheet, so ours start with `wsw-`. The panel's `renderOrder` is 1.2: UIKit's transparent panels sort ahead of the paper wall (order 1) unless told otherwise, and 1.2 keeps the fingertip (hand visual, 1.5) drawn over it. Twelve settings do not fit one panel at a size a fingertip can use (it would be 1 m tall), so there are two pages of six, with a page button. The panel is built on first open and only shown and hidden after that, and is `PokeInteractable` only while open, so a hidden panel catches nothing.
7. **Placement** (`placement.ts`, pure and tested): 0.5 m ahead of the head, 0.3 m below the eyes and tilted up to the face, shifted 10 cm toward the main hand. **Both hands always work**: the main hand only sets which side the panel opens on, so a one-handed player is never locked out and a stray brush by the other hand is a minor risk we accepted.
8. **High contrast** is `highContrast` in the store plus listeners: the stand-in wall (`TestStageView`) becomes pure white, fully lit and opaque, with a black border, the shadows fully solid (`ShadowRenderer.baseOpacity` 1), and the panel black on white with black outlines (`PALETTE` in `markup.ts`). The wall has no texture today; **the room art (Teammate B) must read `settings.value.highContrast` and drop its paper texture and warm tint.** The hand glove keeps its own dark look.
9. **Captions are a stub:** the setting exists, is saved and has its row ("coming with the story"), and nothing displays them until story beats exist.

## Consequences

- Opening the panel: the Hand Lab's Settings button on a desktop, the launcher's Testing button ("Settings panel opens when VR starts", a debug tool, [ADR 0012](0012-debug-tools.md)) in a headset for now (and `__wsw.openSettings()`); in play, nothing opens it yet. Teammate B's lantern flow should call `openSettings` (a palm-up gesture or a gear by the lantern were considered).
- A new setting is a table entry, a page slot and, if it steers an engine object, one case in `SettingsStore.apply`; a new field also needs a save version bump.
- Settings are per browser, like the save.
- **Verified:** the whole flow in headless Chromium (`scripts/settings-probe.mjs`: opens, both pages, every kind of control, values reach the recognizer, projector and mixer, high contrast, a reload keeps them, reset, close), the saves migration and merge, `npm run check`.
- **Not verified:** that a fingertip poke presses these buttons in a headset (the probe sends a click event, as a desktop mouse does), on the Pico (Wolvic Chromium) or a Quest; the touch pointer's distances (`TOUCH_DEFAULTS`) against 64 px (4 cm) buttons; that the panel reads well at 0.5 m in a headset. If a poke does nothing, or IWSDK's panels behave differently on a device, stop and decide between tuning the touch pointer and our own fingertip hit test.
