# The story: one night, one friendship

A boy has recently lost his best friend, a girl from down the street. He wakes in the night, lights a candle, and in shadow play on his bedroom wall he goes back through the friendship they shared, from the night they met to the last ordinary day. At the end he can sleep again. The premise and the tone rules are in the [GDD](GDD.md#the-story); this document is the scene-by-scene design.

**Status:** every scene is a first draft. We work on them one at a time: build a scene, play it in the headset, and rewrite its section here so it says how the scene is now. The order, the shapes and the content of any scene can change.

## One story, not seven

The player should feel they are watching one puppet show that runs from the candle being lit to the candle going out, with seven things to do in it, not seven short films. Four things hold it together:

The friend is a girl, so the two kids read apart at a glance: her puppet has a bow and a ponytail, and a dress when she stands (`girl` in `story-kit.ts`); the boy has a tuft of hair and a jumper and shorts. The story shows them as best friends and leaves anything more to the player's reading.

1.  **One friendship, told in order.** The memories follow the friendship through a year: they meet on a stormy winter night, play in the park in spring, catch fireflies in summer, and walk to school in autumn. Each memory starts where the last one left off, often with the same two kids walking or running from one into the next.
2.  **The friend teaches every shape.** The friend taught the boy his first shadow rabbit, and every shape after it is the friend saying "now try this one". The friend's hand that shows each shape comes out of the memory itself: the friend's puppet raises her hand, and the big shadow of her hand is lifted up onto the wall from below, her arm trailing off its bottom edge. Making the shapes she taught is how the boy remembers her.
3.  **Threads that come back.** A few things recur so the ending pays off what came before:
    - **The wave.** The friend waves in the dream that wakes the boy (scene 1). At the school gate she turns back and waves once more, and the boy, already walking away, doesn't see it (scene 5). In scene 6 the same hand is raised alone on the wall, and this time the boy answers it, palm to palm. The night has been about returning that wave.
    - **The rabbit and the fox.** The rabbit is the boy's shape, the fox is the friend's. They meet in the fort, chase each other into the park, and lie together in its grass under the fireflies. In scene 5, the rabbit goes one way and the fox the other.
    - **The jar.** The jar from the firefly night was the friend's, and she gives it to the boy. It is on the boy's shelf in the room from the start, unlit, and the last of the candle finds it in scene 7.
    - **The storm.** They met on a stormy night, and the storm that wakes the boy is like that one. Thunder outside connects the room and the memory.
    - **A hand over the light.** In the fort on the night they met, footsteps come and the girl claps her hand over the torch, and everything holds its breath until they pass (scene 2). It is how the player learns, without a word, what covering the candle means: hush, everything waits. That is what it does for six scenes (it pauses the game), and in scene 7 the same gesture lets him sleep.
4.  **A puppet stage, not a slideshow.** After the candle is lit, the wall never goes black and the story never fades out and in again. Scenes change the way a shadow theatre changes them: the cut-outs of one place slide off one side of the wall while the next place slides in from the other, and the kids walk through the change from one into the next (see [Scene changes](#scene-changes)).

## Rules for every scene

- **Show, don't tell.** No dialogue and no narration. At most a word or two written on the wall where a picture cannot do the job. Each scene must make sense to a player who reads nothing.
- **The loss is never shown.** No illness, accident, funeral or hospital. It is felt through absence.
- **Bittersweet, not tragic.** The memories are happy and ordinary; the sadness is that they are memories.
- **No failure.** A scene waits as long as it takes. The friend's hand always shows the shape to make.
- **Arms rest while a scene plays.** The player only holds up a shape between memories, while the story waits.
- **A memory is short:** about 30 to 60 seconds of puppet theatre, plus a scene change of a few seconds into the next.

## How the story moves on: the friend's hand

Each memory (scenes 2 to 5) ends on a picture that leads into the next one, and the story waits there:

1.  **The friend asks.** Inside the memory, the friend's puppet lifts her hand, and the friend's hand shadow comes up large from below the wall, loose, and makes the next shape as it arrives. It never fades in out of nothing: it is a real hand coming up into the candle's light, its forearm always running off the bottom of the wall, so it is held up from somewhere, never floating. It is softer and slightly lighter than the player's, so the two are never confused, and it holds the shape gently and repeats it, as if it were waiting. The memory's last picture stays alive behind it, breathing (a torch flickering, grass swaying), so the story is paused, not stopped.
2.  **The player copies it.** When the player's shape commits, the friend's hand lowers back down out of sight below the wall. The wall shows the player, without a word, that copying is what's wanted and how close they are (`shape-guide.ts`):
    - **She copies first (scene 2 only).** Her hand rises open and, for about five seconds of the player's hand being seen, copies it: it follows the player's hand about the wall and makes whichever painted shape the player makes (an open hand otherwise). Then it drifts home and makes the rabbit. Hands answer hands, before any shape is asked for (`FriendBeat.mirror`).
    - **An outline to fill.** While her hand waits, a faint outline of her shape stands still beside it, toward the middle of the wall, where his matching hand would go, mirrored so it faces hers, as the two kids face each other. It is a thin line just outside the shape (her painted hand's parts joined, so only its own edge is drawn), a frame for the player's shadow to fit. It darkens a little as the hand gets closer. It is only a place to aim: the shape counts anywhere on the wall.
    - **Warmer, colder.** The candle steadies and burns up a little as the player's hand nears her shape (the classifier's margin on it), and flickers as usual when it's far off.
    - **She's waiting.** Once the shape is recognised and being held, her hand leans a few centimetres toward the player's, so a player who has it right keeps holding it.
3.  **The shadow joins the story.** The player's shadow becomes a puppet in the picture that's already there (the fox pounces on the rabbit, the cup holds a firefly), and that puppet carries the story into the next place.

Scene 2 is the only memory that plays before its friend's hand: a short prologue, the night they met, shows who she is and puts the fort on the wall, and her hand rises over it once she has raised her own. Scene 6 is the friend's hand with nothing behind it, and that emptiness is the point.

This is the tutorial, the help for a stuck player, and the friend's presence in one.

## Scene changes

Like a shadow theatre with rod puppets on a track:

- **Places slide, they don't fade.** A place (the fort, the park, the school gate) is a group of cut-outs. When the story moves on, the old place slides off one side of the wall, eased, as the new one slides in from the other, a little behind it, so the wall is never bare for long. Pieces at different depths slide at slightly different speeds (near ones faster), so it reads as the stage turning and not as a picture being swapped.
- **Time moves left to right.** Moving forward in the friendship, places leave to the left and arrive from the right, and the kids walk or run rightwards through the change. Once, in scene 5, the park slides back in from the left, the wrong way, because the story has stopped going forward.
- **Characters carry over.** The two kids (and the rabbit and fox) stay on the wall through a change and walk into the next place, so the player follows them, not a cut.
- **The light changes with the place.** During a change the candle's mood eases to the next memory's (`StoryScene.candle`), and the candle burns a little lower.
- **The only fades** are the dream in scene 1, the friend's hand leaving in scene 6, and the final dark in scene 7.

**How it is built:** the story director (`src/engine/stage/story/story-director.ts`) plays each scene as a list of beats in `story-script.ts`: a `friend` beat shows the friend's hand and waits for the player's commit of its shape; a `memory` beat plays a puppet scene (keyframed tracks, `src/engine/stage/puppet/`) and can start one of its puppets where the player's shadow was. The friend's hand is a painted shadow of a hand making the shape, drawn the way the old shadow-puppet plates draw one (`friend-puppet.ts`, its art in `puppet-art.ts`), as a small puppet with the moving fingers (a rabbit's ears) as parts of their own; it forms the shape, holds it, and every few seconds eases half out of it and back. (A posed hand cast like the player's was tried first: a shadow made of a hand's bones reads as a hand, not as a rabbit.) Each shape needs its own painted hand; a shape without one shows nothing yet. The story is written for the 1.6 by 1 m stage and drawn `STORY_SCALE` (2.375) times bigger, centred on the wall's middle, so the memories, the friend's hand and the story's words fill 95% of the 4 by 2.5 m wall (`StageFrame.setStoryArea`); the player's own shadows keep their size. The candle's light has a mood (`candle-mood.ts`): the story opens `rainy`, its light thin and blue-grey, and a scene can name another (`StoryScene.candle`, e.g. `warm`) for the light to ease into over a few seconds, so the candle's colour can mark the story's moments; the candle's own flame, and the light on its wax, take the mood's colour too (`CandleMoodBlend.flame`, the mood's light over `warm`'s). `__wsw.candleMood('warm')` tries one. In dev, `__wsw.playStory()` starts the story and `__wsw.commitShape('rabbit')` copies the shape without a hand; `node scripts/story-probe.mjs` takes screenshots.

**The continuous stage:** the memories are chapters of one puppet scene (`src/engine/stage/puppet/chapters.ts` joins them; `story-stage.ts` lists them), so a puppet in several memories is one part that carries over. A `memory` beat plays one chapter (`start`, `end`); when it ends, its last picture stays on the wall with its wobbles still moving (`ScenePlayer.idle`) through the next `friend` beat, and the next chapter goes on from there. `slideKeys` writes a place's slide on or off, slower the further back it stands, and the puppets are cut off at the room wall's edges (`PuppetView.clipToWall`). The friend's hand rises into place from below the wall (`FRIEND_FROM_Y`) and lowers back there once copied; each painted hand has the rest of her arm (`friend-arm`) running off the wall's bottom, and the wall's edge cuts it off as it does the puppets. A beat's `leave: 'fade'` fades it where it is instead (scene 6). `node scripts/story-probe.mjs <url> <prefix> [1,4] [0.3,3] '[["fox",[0.6,3,7]]]'` follows the story into later scenes (`PROBE_WIDE=1` for the whole view).

**All the scenes:** scenes 3 to 5 are chapters too (`the-park.ts`, `fireflies.ts`, `last-ordinary-day.ts`); the kids, sitting or standing, and their walk and run come from `story-kit.ts`. A beat can also be a `wait` (seconds of nothing new) or `sleep` (scene 7: covering the flame ends the game instead of pausing it; the candle goes out and the view fades to black). A friend beat can name which painted hand shows its shape (`puppet`, e.g. `wave`) and how slowly it goes (`out`). A memory can change the candle's mood on the way with a `mood` cue; each scene's `candle` is the mood its wait opens in. Starting the story at a later scene puts the picture the memory before it ended on on the wall, so any scene is a checkpoint: the Hand Lab's **Story** tab has one per scene (Wait shows the friend's hand, Play copies its shape at once), with Make shape (the Lab's synthetic hand forms the waiting shape, through the real recognition, even when the session started on the device's hands), Copy shape and Skip memory; pressed before BEGIN, a checkpoint starts the session itself; in dev, `__wsw.jumpTo(3, true)` does the same. `node scripts/scene-probe.mjs <url> <dir> '[[1,[2,8,14]]]'` jumps to scenes and takes screenshots.

## The scenes

| # | Scene | Season | Shape | Leads into the next with |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| 1 | Waking | the stormy night now | none (light the candle) | The candle's light on the wall, like the torch in the fort |
| 2 | How they met | winter, a stormy night | Rabbit | The friend makes a fox with the torch: "now this one" |
| 3 | The park | spring | Fox | Running into the evening; the first firefly |
| 4 | Fireflies | summer | Cup | The friend gives him the jar; they walk on towards school |
| 5 | The last ordinary day | autumn | Flat (a wave) | The friend's wave the boy didn't see; the park comes back with one boy in it |

Fist and spread are not used: a fist reads as anger and is the easiest shape to make by accident, and spread has no memory that fits it yet.

### 1. Waking

- **Purpose:** set the mood without a word. He is awake, alone and it is late. Plant the wave the story will end on.
- **What happens:** BEGIN fades the title to black (built). The headset stays black. In it, his friend slowly gathers: a faint pale glow of a girl lifting a hand to wave, soft-edged and never quite clear, like a dream (built). Then far off thunder rolls, low and muffled, and the dream goes with it: it is what woke him (built). While it rumbles away his eyes open on the room: a slow, heavy blink, a quicker, wider one, and they stay open (built). After the second blink he sits up: the covers slide down to his lap with a soft rustle of cloth (built). He is in his bed: the rumpled quilt runs from a fold at his waist over his knees and feet to the bed's foot, the pillow and the sheet where he lay are behind him, and the tray across his lap holds the candle (built). The room is dark and cold blue. A short silence, then the room tone. On the shelf, half seen, is a glass jar. The player lights the candle (built), and the room warms a little.
- **The player:** lights the candle.
- **Leads into scene 2:** the candle throws its light on the wall, and the night they met begins in it: his room in the same storm.
- **Must make the player feel:** lonely, then a little safer once the light is there.
- **Built already:** the fade to black, the blinks (`src/engine/stage/wake-timeline.ts` has the timing, `src/engine/shadow/wake-view.ts` draws it; `__wsw.playWake()` replays it), the cold room, lighting the candle.
- **Lighting it (`src/engine/stage/ignition.ts`):** the match's flame held within 15 cm of the wick for 3 s lights it. From 35 cm the wick answers the match: its glow brightens, grows and pulses faster, and the match's flame leans toward it and grows a little. If the match drifts off, the progress holds for 0.8 s and then drains slowly (about a third as fast as it fills), so a shaky hand doesn't have to start again.
- **In the room:** the jar (`glassJar.glb`, piece `jar` in `room-models.ts`) stands unlit on top of the bookcase behind the player's right shoulder, her torch lying beside it; moving boxes, folded blankets and a toy fox dress the room.
- **To try:** whether the friend in the dream reads as a friend and never as a figure to fear (keep it small, soft and brief); `__wsw.playWake()` replays it. A whispered "noo…" was tried in the dark and dropped (it read wrong); the timeline still has a slot for a word. Whether the dream's wave should match the friend's flat hand in scene 6 closely enough to be recognised.

### 2. How they met

- **Purpose:** introduce the friend and the game's one mechanic, and start the friendship.
- **Prologue (the night they met):** before any shape, the wall shows how they met, about 17.6 s. His room slides in: a window with rain on the glass over two moving boxes (he is new here), and the boy sitting alone on the floor, head down. Lightning in the puppet window, and the room's real window flashes with it; the thunder follows 1.5 s later, the same storm as the room's. He flinches and pulls his blanket over his head, and the heap trembles. A torch beam comes in at the left edge, and behind it the girl from down the street, walking, sweeping the light. She kneels by the heap and sets the torch down; it clicks on, warm (`click`). She throws the blanket up off him, and the fort comes down from above the wall over them both. He looks up at her, and she raises her hand. The friend's rabbit hand rises over it. It is `the-storm.ts`; the room (window, boxes) slides off with the fort in scene 3. The `storm` cue (`puppet-scene.ts`) is how a memory strikes the room's storm (`Storm.strikeNow`).
- **What happens:** at the wall's left, beside the girl's raised hand, the friend's hand makes a rabbit. When the player copies it, the player's rabbit hops off their hand and becomes a puppet rabbit in the fort, two small kids under a blanket with a torch on a stormy night, one showing the other how to make the rabbit. The new kid tries, gets it wrong (a lumpy shape), then gets it right. They both laugh. Then footsteps creak outside, and they hush the torch (see **The hush**, below). When the footsteps have gone, the friend, pleased with herself, turns her hand in the torchlight into something new.
- **The player:** Rabbit. (Nothing to do in the hush: it is shown, not asked for.)
- **Ends on (the wait for scene 3):** the fort stays, the torch flickering and the boy's rabbit sitting on the blanket; the friend's puppet raises her hand, and the friend's hand shadow rises at the wall's edge making a fox.
- **Must make the player feel:** delight at the transformation; warmth towards the friend.
- **Puppets:** the boy, the friend, the blanket fort, the torch, a rabbit, and in the hush a thin line of light from a door opening a crack.
- **The hush:**
  - **Why:** covering the flame pauses the game, but nothing has told the player so, and making them pause as a lesson would turn a comfort into a chore. Here the friend shows what a hand over the light means, inside the memory, and the player only watches.
  - **What happens (about 7 s, after the laugh and before her fox):** soft footsteps creak somewhere outside the fort (a grown-up up late, not a threat). A thin line of light opens at the wall's right edge, a door left ajar. The girl claps her hand over the torch, quick and flat, and the fort goes dark but for the line of light. At the same moment **the real candle dims**, down to about 40% for as long as her hand is on the torch, so the player feels the light in their own room held too. The two kids freeze, heads up. The line of light lingers, narrows and closes; the footsteps go away. She lifts her hand, the torch comes back (and the candle with it, over about half a second), and the two of them shake with giggles, heads tipped together, as kids do when they have got away with something.
  - **How it reads:** playful, never frightening. No voice and no grown-up shown: footsteps and a line of light are enough. Her hand covers the torch the way a hand covers the candle, cupped flat over it from above, so the player can see the likeness.
  - **How it is built:** in `how-they-met.ts`, after the rabbit has hopped down and before her arm rises for the fox (the chapter is 28 s). At the first footsteps the rabbit scampers off the torch to the boy (it sat on it) and crouches, ears back; it hops back to its place in the giggles, where the fox finds it. She scoots over, comes up onto her knees and lunges, her hand coming down just over the torch (`leaningHand` works out the reach; a test holds the drawn hand there). The door's line of light is the `door-light` glow. Footsteps are `step` cues (the placeholder footsteps, captioned "[footsteps outside]"); `hush` cues ease the room's candle light to 40% in 0.2 s and back in 0.5 s (`light-hush.ts`), and a skipped memory puts it back at once.
  - **To try:** whether players link her hand on the torch to their hand on the candle; whether the candle dimming is enough of a link, or too subtle; that nobody thinks it asks them to do something.
- **Note:** the rabbit is slow to commit and the classifier is weakest on it, so accept it generously here. It is the first shape, and it must not frustrate.
- **How it is built (`src/engine/stage/story/how-they-met.ts`, the cut-outs in `src/engine/shadow/puppet-art.ts`):** the fort and the kids are already there from the prologue, so nothing slides in. The friend's rabbit hand rises at the wall's left, beyond the girl (`x -0.5, y 0.17`), clear of the fort's edge so it doesn't hide the kids. The player's rabbit becomes a puppet rabbit that hops to it at once; then come the boy's lumpy try, the friend leaning over to help, the boy's rabbit coming right, the rabbits nodding to each other, and both laughing. A far thunder rolls in the memory while she shows him the rabbit (`rumble`, from the memory's window). At the end, after the hush, the boy lowers his hand, the rabbit hops down onto the blanket between them, and the friend's arm goes down and up again; the story holds that picture while the friend's fox hand rises over it at the wall's left, as her rabbit did.
- **To try:** whether the friend's painted rabbit hand reads in a headset, and whether players copy it with one hand; the sizes on the wall in a headset; whether the laughter (`laugh`, after his rabbit comes right and again in the giggles after the hush) sits well under the music.

### 3. The park

- **Purpose:** pure joy. The friendship at its most carefree.
- **What happens:** the player's fox pounces at the rabbit, and the rabbit bolts out of the fort. The fort slides off to the left as trees slide in from the right, and the chase runs on through the change: rabbit and fox across the wall, out of winter and into a spring park. Mid-run they become the two kids, the girl chasing the boy between the trees, until she catches him. Out of breath, they slow to a stop and lie down together in the grass, side by side, looking up. The sky goes from day to dusk (the candle eases warmer and lower), the stars come out one by one, and a single small light blinks above them.
- **The player:** Fox.
- **Ends on (the wait for scene 4):** the two kids lying in the grass, the firefly drifting over them; the friend's puppet reaches up, and her hand shadow rises cupped, in the sky between the left and middle trees; the outline of the cup stands in the gap between the middle and right trees' crowns; the left tree's crown would hide it at the wall's left.
- **Must make the player feel:** play, speed, laughter.
- **Puppets:** the fox, trees, the stars, the kids running and lying down (reused from scene 2), one firefly.
- **How it is built (`src/engine/stage/story/the-park.ts`, about 25 s):** the friend's painted fox hand; the player's fox lands in the fort, crouches and pounces, and the rabbit bolts. The two run on the spot while the fort and the kids slide off to the left and the trees and grass slide in from the right (the candle warming); mid-run the rabbit and the fox cross-fade into the two kids running, the friend chasing. She tags him and slows to a stop; he stumbles on a few steps and stops just beyond her. Each sits down gently on the grass, legs stretched out in front, takes a moment to look up, and then leans slowly back until they lie on their backs, heads tipped to the sky, the boy's head just past her feet (no tumbling, no sliding, no tipping over stiff as a plank). They giggle softly (two slow heaves). The light goes to dusk, and the stars come out one by one: nine small, pale, still lights high in the sky behind the trees (`STARS`), clear of where the firefly hovers and the friend's cupped hand rises; a firefly, green-gold and wandering, is a different thing. A firefly drifts in to hover between them; the friend reaches up, and her cup hand rises. The stars stay through scene 4's night and go out as its morning comes.
- **To try:** the rabbit and fox becoming the kids is a cross-fade; the two lie in a row, not head to head; whether the stars read as stars and never as more fireflies.
- **Sound:** laughter (`laugh`, as they lie in the grass).

### 4. Fireflies

- **Purpose:** a quiet, close moment. The most tender memory.
- **What happens:** when the player copies the cup, the firefly settles into their cupped shadow and glows there, then goes back to hover over the two kids, still lying in the grass. More fireflies come up out of the grass; the boy reaches up beside her, and the fireflies come down one at a time to settle on their two raised hands, which glow with them. They open their hands and let the fireflies go, a few at a time, up into the sky. All but the first, the player's, which lingers over them. They sit up in the grass, turned to each other; the friend takes out a jar, the lingering firefly comes down into it, and she holds it out, glowing; the boy takes it from her hand into his lap. In the morning light of the walk to school its glow can no longer be seen, and in scene 5 the jar is dark. They stand and walk on together, to the right.
- **The player:** Cup.
- **Ends on (the wait for scene 5):** as they walk, the park slides off to the left and a school gate slides in from the right; it is morning, and autumn leaves fall. At the gate the boy turns round to face her; the friend's puppet, facing him, lifts her hand, and the friend's hand shadow rises flat, waving.
- **Must make the player feel:** stillness, closeness, a summer that seemed endless.
- **Puppets:** the kids sitting (one shot, to reuse in scene 5), a jar, fireflies (small lights on the wall), the school gate (arriving).
- **How it is built (`src/engine/stage/story/fireflies.ts`, about 43 s):** the firefly comes off the player's cup glowing brighter and goes back over the kids, lying in the park; eight fireflies rise from its grass and the boy reaches up beside her; the fireflies come down one at a time, turn and turn about, to their raised hands, each hand's glow brightening with each; they let them go in threes over the top of the wall, all but the first (the player's), which lingers over her, and their arms come down; they sit up in the grass facing each other (lying kids cross-fade to sitting ones), the friend takes out the jar, the firefly comes down into it, she holds it out and the boy takes it from her hand into his lap (the jar hangs from each kid's hand, so it is always in it). They stand and walk on, the boy carrying the jar, walking on the spot while the ground goes by under them: the grass, the trees and then the school gate and an autumn tree all slide past to the left at the pace of their stride (`groundKeys`), so their feet never skate, and the stars go out as the light turns to a pale morning. The ground comes to rest as they stop at the gate, and five leaves fall; the boy turns round at the gateway to face the friend, she raises her hand, and her waving hand rises beside her. There is no walk to a hill and no filling of the jar: walking off to sit somewhere else read as unmotivated.
- **To try:** the firefly light comes from the wall, not the candle, so this is the only scene with a second light. Check that it reads as magic, not as a bug. Whether the jar being handed over reads at this size.

### 5. The last ordinary day

- **Purpose:** the turn. Without saying it, show that this was the last time.
- **What happens:** when the player waves, the boy waves back at the gate, face to face, as on any day, and they turn and go opposite ways: the boy off the right edge of the wall, the friend off the left. Just before the edge, the friend turns back once and waves again, but the boy's puppet is already gone. The wave hangs there, unanswered, and then the friend walks off too. The gate slides away, and the hill slides back in, from the left, the wrong way: the hill and the jar from scene 4, with only the boy sitting there.
- **The player:** Flat (a wave).
- **Ends on (the wait for scene 6):** the park slides away, slowly, until the wall is empty for the first time since the candle was lit.
- **Must make the player feel:** that something is ending, before they can say what.
- **Puppets:** the school gate, the kids walking and waving, the single boy sitting in the park's grass (from scene 4).
- **How it is built (`src/engine/stage/story/last-ordinary-day.ts`, about 35 s):** the boy waves back with his free hand and walks off the right edge with the jar; the friend walks left, turns back near the edge and waves at nobody, holds her hand up, lowers it, and walks off; the gate slides away to the right as the park slides back in from the left, the light cooling to `low`, with only the boy sitting in its grass, turned to where the friend sat, the jar dark in the grass in front of him; he bows his head, and the park slides slowly off to the right, leaving the wall empty.
- **How it lands without words:** the wave that isn't seen, the park coming back the wrong way with the girl missing, the laughter from scene 3 gone, and the candle dimming. This is the scene to test hardest with someone who doesn't know the premise.

### 6. Goodbye

- **Purpose:** let the boy, and the player, say goodbye: answer the wave.
- **What happens:** no stage. The wall is empty except for the friend's hand, held up flat, alone: the wave from the gate, and from the dream. It waits. When the player raises their own flat hand to meet it, palm to palm, the two shadows touch; the joined hands turn clockwise, face on to side on, as the view draws back, and show what they are: two hands pressed palm to palm, the boy's from the left and the girl's from the right, the two kids seen from the waist up, each reaching out to the touch. They stay so a while. Then their palms part and their arms come down; she fades away, and he, alone, bows his head and fades too.
- **The player:** Flat, held against the friend's hand.
- **Must make the player feel:** grief, and some peace.
- **Puppets:** the friend's hand; then the two kids from the waist up (the standing kids, big), with side-on hands.
- **How it is built (`src/engine/stage/story/goodbye.ts`, about 18.5 s):** the friend's painted flat hand (`flat`, its thumb easing out as it opens) waits alone in the middle of the empty wall; any flat commit answers it (it is not checked to be palm to palm). ### 7. Sleep

- **Purpose:** the ending: rest.
- **What happens:** the candle has burned low. The wall is dim and the room quiet; the rain is easing. The last of the candle's light finds the jar on the shelf, the one the friend gave him. The player covers the flame, and it goes out. Darkness, the boy's breathing slows, and the game fades to black.
- **The player:** covers the flame. In every other scene this pauses the game; here it puts the candle out. The player chooses when he sleeps. It is the gesture she showed him in the fort in scene 2, hushing the torch: there it made everything wait, and here it lets him rest.
- **Echoing the hush (`sleep-hint.ts`):** the ghost hand that shows the cover (below) moves the way she covered the torch in scene 2: a quick, flat clap over the flame, held, then lifted. The flame dips under it as the torch went dark, so the player recognises the gesture. It stays a pale child's hand, not hers: her hand left in scene 6, and bringing it back here would undo the goodbye. Each cover: it gathers faintly above the flame (1 s), claps down in 0.22 s, holds 1.6 s, lifts away over 1.3 s and rests 2.4 s; while it covers the flame the room's light dips by 35%.
- **Must make the player feel:** calm and finished.
- **The light:** it eases to `ember`, and after 3 s covering the flame ends the game (the `asleep` state). The jar stands on the shelf (see scene 1), and there is a yawn (`yawn`, 10.2 s into the end, as his eyes come back open after the first heavy blink).
- **The end (`src/engine/stage/ending-timeline.ts` has the timing):** covering the flame does not cut to a fade. The candle goes out slowly, the opening's lighting backwards: the flame shrinks from the size it had under the hand to nothing and its light on the room ebbs from where the cover left it over 6.5 s (a hand no longer changes it), and the moon sinks from its night level to half. A moment in the dark, moonlit room, then his eyes get heavy: the opening's lids (`wake-view.ts`) close nearly shut, open to 0.7, close again, open a last time to 0.35, and stay shut (17.6 s). The rain eases throughout and the music fades with the candle. Over the black the credits roll (`src/ui/credits/`, the team and the third-party credits from CREDITS.md), hanging still in front of where he was looking, with the music back softly under them; then "Thank you for playing" alone, and at 56.5 s the session ends and the title page is there again. `__wsw.playEnding()` plays it from anywhere in the story, and so does covering the flame with the Developer overlay's **Covering ends the story (test)** switched on; `node scripts/ending-probe.mjs <url> <dir>` steps through it and prints the light, lids, credits and sound.
- **Showing that covering is wanted (`src/engine/stage/story/sleep-hint.ts`):** for six scenes covering the flame has paused the game, and her hand is gone, so the end shows it without a word. A faint, pale ghost of a cupped hand comes down over the candle, holds over the flame and lifts away, about every 6.6 s (`hands/cover-hand.ts` poses it, `render/ghost-hand-view.ts` draws it). It grows stronger from 20 to 30 s into the wait, and gives way while the player's own hand is at the flame. After 40 s, and every 60 s after that, "goodnight" is written on the wall. Covering looks like putting the candle out, not pausing: there is no pause ring; the flame bows under a near hand (from 30 cm, not the usual 16) and shrinks, and its light dims, as the cover goes on (`CandleView.sleepMode`).
- **To try:** whether the ghost hand reads as "do this" and not as someone else's hand in the room; whether "goodnight" is needed at all; the breathing or a small yawn as a sound cue instead. Whether the ghost should be her hand after all (softer and lighter, like the friend's hand in the other scenes), one last time: it would keep "the friend's hand always shows the shape", but it may weaken scene 6's goodbye.
- **To try:** whether one firefly glows in the jar after the candle goes out (magic, and maybe too much); whether the jar is enough without it.

## Throughout the night

- **The candle burns lower** at each scene change, so the player can see the night moving towards sleep. Its mood follows the memories (winter blue in the fort, warm in the park, dusk gold under the fireflies, pale morning at the gate) and cools again towards the end.
- **The rain** falls all night outside the window, and its sound is a steady bed from the window's side. Now and then the window flickers pale and, a few seconds later, thunder rolls far off, low and muffled: safe inside on a wet night, never a threat (`src/engine/stage/storm.ts` times it; `__wsw.thunder()` brings a flash now). It is the same weather as the night they met, and it eases off in scene 7 as he nears sleep.
- **The music** is one quiet, bittersweet track ("I Got Lost" by Migfus20), looped from the candle's lighting through every scene change. It goes out with the candle as he falls asleep and comes back softly under the credits.
- **Puppets in total:** about eight (the boy and the girl, a rabbit, a fox, a blanket fort, the park's trees and grass, a school gate, a jar and fireflies), each with simple rod-puppet motion: slides, bobs and turns on one pivot; and one real jar in the room.
